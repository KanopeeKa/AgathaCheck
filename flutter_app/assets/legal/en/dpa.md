# Data Processing Addendum

**Effective date:** 21 July 2026

This Data Processing Addendum (“DPA”) forms part of the Agreement between you (“Customer” or “Controller”) and **SAS McCauley Conseils**, trading as **Agatha Track** (“Processor”), governing the processing of personal data under the Agreement. In the event of any conflict between this DPA and the Agreement, this DPA shall prevail with respect to data protection matters.

## 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this DPA:

- “Agreement” means the Terms of Use and any other agreement governing Customer’s use of Agatha Track services.  
- “Applicable data protection law” means the EU GDPR, the UK GDPR, the Data Protection Act 2018 (UK), and any other applicable national data protection legislation.  
- “EU GDPR” means Regulation (EU) 2016/679.  
- “UK GDPR” means the UK General Data Protection Regulation as defined in section 3(10) of the Data Protection Act 2018 (as amended).  
- “Controller”, “Processor”, “Personal data”, “Processing”, “Data subject”, “Member State”, “Supervisory authority” and other GDPR terms have the meanings given in the EU GDPR, or, where the context requires, the UK GDPR.  
- “Standard Contractual Clauses” or “SCCs” means the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses for transfers of personal data to third countries, as adopted under Article 46(2) of the EU GDPR.

## 2. Scope and purpose

This DPA applies where Customer uses Agatha Track services and Processor processes Personal data on behalf of Customer as a Processor. It sets out the subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of such Processing, the types of Personal data, categories of Data subjects, and the rights and obligations of the Controller, as required by Article 28(3) of the EU GDPR and Article 28(3) of the UK GDPR.

Processor shall process Personal data only:
- on documented instructions from Controller, as set out in the Agreement and this DPA;  
- for the purposes of providing the Agatha Track services, including pet and foster management, reminders, collaboration, organisation administration, security, backups, and support;  
- in compliance with Applicable data protection law.

Controller is responsible for determining the purposes and means of Processing and for ensuring that the Processing complies with Applicable data protection law, including having a lawful basis for any data entered about staff, volunteers, fosters, external fosters, and other contacts.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities

Controller is responsible for:
- determining the purposes and means of Processing;  
- ensuring that instructions to Processor are lawful and that Processor’s processing is authorised under Applicable data protection law;  
- informing Data subjects about the Processing in accordance with Articles 13 and 14 of the EU GDPR and Articles 13 and 14 of the UK GDPR, except where Processor has a direct legal obligation to inform;  
- responding to Data subject requests, except where Processor is required by law to respond directly, in which case Processor shall promptly inform Controller and cooperate as reasonably requested.

Processor is responsible for:
- Processing Personal data only on Controller’s documented instructions as set out in the Agreement and this DPA;  
- implementing appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, as described in Article 32 of the EU GDPR and Article 32 of the UK GDPR;  
- assisting Controller, taking into account the nature of the Processing, in complying with Controller’s obligations regarding security, breach notification, data protection impact assessments, and prior consultation with Supervisory authorities.

## 4. Types of data and categories of data subjects

Without limiting the generality of the Agreement, the types of Personal data Processed may include:
- contact and account data (for example name, email address, optional phone number and address);  
- operational data related to pets, foster placements, care routines, medications, reminders, logs, and history;  
- notes and records created by Controller or its users in connection with the service;  
- technical and security data (logs, IP addresses, device information) as described in the Privacy Notice.

Categories of Data subjects may include:
- employees, volunteers, administrators, and other users of Controller;  
- fosters, external fosters, adopters, volunteers, and other contacts managed by Controller;  
- other individuals whose data Controller chooses to enter into the service, in compliance with Applicable data protection law.

## 5. Special categories of data

Controller shall not, and shall ensure that its users do not, intentionally upload special categories of Personal data (Article 9 EU GDPR / Article 9 UK GDPR) to the service, unless strictly necessary for Controller’s purposes and compliant with Applicable data protection law. This includes data revealing health information beyond basic care logs, genetic or biometric data, or data concerning criminal convictions, except where strictly necessary and lawful.

Processor does not actively monitor content for special categories of data and relies on Controller to ensure that any such data uploaded is lawful and necessary. Controller acknowledges that use of free-text notes and document uploads may inadvertently capture sensitive information and remains responsible for minimising and lawfully managing such data.

## 6. Processor obligations

Processor shall:
- process Personal data only on Controller’s documented instructions, including with regard to transfers of Personal data to a third country;  
- ensure that persons authorised to process Personal data are subject to a duty of confidentiality or appropriate contractual obligation;  
- take appropriate security measures as required by Article 32 of the EU GDPR and Article 32 of the UK GDPR;  
- not engage another processor (“sub-processor”) without prior specific or general written authorisation of Controller in accordance with Section 7;  
- assist Controller in responding to requests from Data subjects exercising their rights;  
- assist Controller in ensuring compliance with security, breach notification, data protection impact assessment, and prior consultation obligations;  
- at Controller’s choice, delete or return all Personal data to Controller at the end of the provision of services, unless Union or Member State law or UK law requires storage, in which case Processor shall securely restrict further processing to the minimum necessary;  
- make available to Controller all information reasonably necessary to demonstrate compliance with this Section 6 and allow for and contribute to audits and inspections as set out in Section 8.

## 7. Sub-processors

Controller grants general authorisation to Processor to engage sub-processors for the provision of the Agatha Track services, subject to the following conditions:

- Processor shall maintain an up-to-date list of sub-processors at [Privacy Notice](/legal/privacy-notice), including the name, location, and purpose of each sub-processor’s engagement.  
- Processor shall notify Controller of any intended addition or replacement of a sub-processor at least 30 days before the change takes effect, by updating the list and, where material, notifying Controller via the service or email.  
- If Controller objects to a new sub-processor on reasonable grounds relating to data protection, Controller may, within 14 days of notification, either:  
  - request that Processor not engage or replace the sub-processor; or  
  - terminate the affected service upon 30 days’ written notice if Processor reasonably determines that it cannot provide the service without the new sub-processor.  
- Processor shall ensure that any sub-processor is bound by written contractual obligations providing data protection safeguards no less protective than those set out in this DPA and complying with Article 28(4) of the EU GDPR and Article 28(4) of the UK GDPR. Processor remains fully liable for the acts and omissions of its sub-processors.

## 8. Audits and inspections

Controller has the right to audit Processor’s compliance with this DPA, subject to the following conditions:

- audits may be conducted no more than once every 12 months, unless a material breach is suspected or a Supervisory authority requires an audit;  
- Controller shall provide at least 30 days’ prior written notice;  
- audits shall be conducted during normal business hours, without undue disruption to Processor’s operations;  
- Controller shall ensure that any auditor is subject to appropriate confidentiality obligations;  
- Controller shall bear the costs of any audit, unless the audit reveals a material breach of this DPA by Processor, in which case Processor shall bear the reasonable direct costs of the audit.

Where multiple Controllers request audits, Processor may offer a shared audit or review of an independent audit report (for example SOC 2 or equivalent) in lieu of individual on-site audits, where reasonably acceptable.

## 9. International transfers

Controller acknowledges that Processor may use infrastructure and sub-processors located in the European Economic Area (EEA), the United Kingdom, and potentially other jurisdictions.

To the extent that Personal data is transferred from the EEA to a country outside the EEA that is not subject to an adequacy decision, the parties agree to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) 2021, incorporated by reference, with the following details:

- Module II (Controller to Processor) applies;  
- Annexes to the SCCs shall be completed by Processor and made available to Controller via the Privacy Notice or a dedicated page;  
- The parties shall implement the technical and organisational measures described in the SCCs and this DPA.

To the extent that Personal data is transferred from the UK to a country outside the UK that is not subject to an adequacy finding under UK law, the parties shall, where required, enter into the International Data Transfer Addendum issued by the UK ICO, or otherwise rely on an appropriate transfer mechanism under UK law, with the EU SCCs as the “Approved Transfer Mechanism” and the UK addendum applied as required.

Nothing in this DPA requires Processor to transfer Personal data to a third country. Processor may keep all primary processing within the EU/EEA and UK, subject to sub-processor arrangements described in the list referenced in Section 7.

## 10. Security and breach notification

Processor shall implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, including measures addressing:
- pseudonymisation and encryption where appropriate;  
- confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems;  
- processes for regularly testing, assessing, and evaluating the effectiveness of security measures;  
- procedures for restoring availability and access to Personal data in a timely manner after an incident.

Without undue delay after becoming aware of a Personal data breach affecting Controller’s data, Processor shall notify Controller of the breach, describing the nature of the breach, the categories and approximate number of Data subjects and Personal data records affected (where known), and the measures taken or proposed to address and mitigate the breach. Processor shall cooperate with Controller in investigating and remedying the breach and in notifying Supervisory authorities and Data subjects where required.

## 11. Data subject requests

Processor shall assist Controller in responding to requests from Data subjects seeking to exercise their rights under Applicable data protection law. Where Processor receives a request directly from a Data subject, Processor shall promptly notify Controller and, unless legally prohibited, direct the Data subject to contact Controller, unless Processor is required by law to respond directly.

## 12. Deletion and return of data

At the end of the provision of services, and subject to applicable law, Processor shall, at Controller’s choice, delete or return all Personal data to Controller, and delete existing copies unless Union or Member State law or UK law requires storage. Where retention is required, Processor shall securely restrict further processing to the minimum necessary for the original purpose of retention.

## 13. Liability

Liability between the parties in relation to data protection matters shall be governed by the Agreement, including any limitation of liability provisions, subject to mandatory provisions of Applicable data protection law. Nothing in this DPA limits or excludes liability that cannot be excluded under Applicable data protection law.

## 14. Term and termination

This DPA shall remain in effect for as long as Processor processes Personal data on behalf of Controller under the Agreement. Either party may terminate the Agreement in accordance with its terms. Sections that by their nature should survive termination (including confidentiality, security, data deletion/return, and liability) shall survive termination of this DPA.

## 15. Governing law and language

This DPA is governed by French law and the language of this DPA is English. A French version of this DPA may be provided for convenience; in the event of any inconsistency between the English and French versions, the French version shall prevail for French-established Controllers, and the English version shall prevail for UK-established Controllers, to the extent permitted by law.

For data protection queries, Controller may contact Processor at [contact@agathatrack.com](mailto:contact@agathatrack.com).
